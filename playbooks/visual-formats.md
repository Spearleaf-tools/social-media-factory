# Visual formats: from polished to raw

> last_researched: 2026-06-11 (as of June 2026)
> Which formats each platform favours is in the platform rulebooks. This file covers the range of
> image formats the Factory can make, when each one fits, and the rotation rule that stops a feed
> looking the same every day. Every example is invented. See `README.md`.

## Why there is a range at all

A feed where every image looks the same becomes wallpaper. Followers stop seeing it. The cure is
not random variety. It is a deliberate range, from polished to raw, with each format chosen for
a reason.

- **Polished** formats say "this is a real, careful business". They build authority and get saved.
- **Raw** formats look like something a person made in a minute. They get noticed, and they feel honest.

The best-looking image is not always the best post. Use both ends on purpose.

## Sizes

> checked: 2026-10-20 (sizes and tall-picture margins only; the rest of this file is as of June 2026)

| Size | Pixels | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Portrait 4:5 | 1080x1350 | Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn. Still the size most guides recommend for carousels. Instagram's profile grid shows a 3:4 crop of it |
| Square 1:1 | 1080x1080 | X, and any place a square is asked for |
| Tall 9:16 | 1080x1920 | TikTok photo posts, Stories, Reels covers. Fills the phone. Chosen automatically for a brand that posts only to TikTok |

A tall picture is seen full screen, and the app draws over it: a bar along the top, the caption
and sound line along the bottom, and a column of buttons down the right. TikTok publishes no
safe-zone figures for ordinary posts, and the guides disagree, so the Factory keeps words out of
the top 240 pixels, the bottom 470 and the right 168, which is on the generous side of them.
Instagram may also accept 3:4 (1080x1440) uploads; that was not confirmed by Instagram itself, so
the Factory does not draw it yet.

Sources: [Revid TikTok safe zone](https://www.revid.ai/tiktok-safe-zone),
[Socialync TikTok safe zones](https://www.socialync.io/tools/safe-zones/tiktok),
[Postfast TikTok carousel sizes](https://postfa.st/sizes/tiktok/carousel),
[Krumzi Instagram carousel size guide](https://www.krumzi.com/blog/instagram-carousel-size-guide).

**Text size.** No word on a picture is smaller than 24 pixels on a 1080-wide picture (about 9
points on a phone at arm's length), whatever the size or format. Grey text, such as a source
line, is held to the same contrast as any small text.

## The formats

**"No key, no photos"** means the format is made entirely from the brand's colours, fonts and
words. It needs no key of any kind.
planner restricts itself to these formats, and every platform can still be served.

| # | Format (name the picture maker uses) | Where it sits | What it is | No key, no photos? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Branded carousel (`carousel`) | Polished | 6 to 10 slides in the brand's colours and fonts: a cover, one idea per slide, a recap, a close | **Yes** |
| 2 | Stat card (`stat`) | Polished | One large number, one line saying what it measures, one line giving its source | **Yes** |
| 3 | Split before and after (`split`) | Polished | Two halves, identically laid out, each with a label and a value: before and after, myth and reality, this and that | **Yes** |
| 4 | Headline card (`headline`, and its siblings `alert` and `bold-text`) | Middle | One bold sentence on a solid brand colour. Nothing else | **Yes** |
| 5 | Note-style card (`notes`) | Raw | Looks like a page from a notes app or a notepad: a title and a few plain lines | **Yes** |
| 6 | Post-screenshot style (`post-screenshot`) | Raw, native | Looks like a short social post: a name, an initial or avatar circle, one or two sharp lines | **Yes** |
| 7 | Photo with text | Raw | A real photo with a short line of text set on it | No. Needs the brand's own approved photos |
| 8 | Plain screenshot | Rawest | A real screenshot or phone photo, untouched or with one arrow: a message, a booking page, the order book | No. Needs a real image supplied and approved by the brand |
| + | LinkedIn PDF (`carousel` with the PDF option on) | Polished | The branded carousel saved as one document, for a LinkedIn document post | **Yes** |

Formats 1 to 6 and the LinkedIn PDF are the **always-available set**. A brand that has chosen
three colours and nothing else (no logo, no photos, no fonts of its own) can make every one of them.

The names in backticks are the ones the Factory's picture maker understands. If this table and
the tool ever disagree, the tool is right: ask Claude to bring this file up to date.

## When each one fits

### 1. Branded carousel
- **Use it for:** teaching. Steps, lists, a process, a breakdown. Anything worth saving.
- **Best on:** Instagram (the workhorse) and LinkedIn (as the PDF).
- **Rules:** cover headline of 5 to 10 words. Slide 2 works alone. One idea per slide, about 12
  words. A recap slide near the end. One soft closing slide. No link on any slide.
- **Watch for:** too many words per slide. If it needs a paragraph, it needs two slides.

### 2. Stat card
- **Use it for:** one real number that changes how the reader sees something.
- **Best on:** all four. Strong as slide 2 of a carousel.
- **Rules:** the number comes from the brand's facts table or proof library, with its source on
  the card. One number per card.
- **Watch for:** a number with no source. If it cannot be traced, there is no stat card.

### 3. Split before and after
- **Use it for:** contrast that reads in a second: before and after, the common belief and the
  truth, the slow way and the quick way.
- **Best on:** Instagram, Facebook, X. Pairs with the `myth-vs-reality` and `before-after-story` shapes.
- **Rules:** both halves laid out identically so that only the difference stands out. Short labels.
  The card is drawn from words and numbers. If you have approved before and after photos, post
  those as a photo with text or as a two-photo album instead.
- **Watch for:** an "after" the brand cannot back up. Both sides must be true.

### 4. Headline card
- **Use it for:** a position, a bold claim, an announcement, an offer.
- **Best on:** Facebook, X and Instagram. On LinkedIn use sparingly.
- **Rules:** one sentence, 12 words or fewer, high contrast, the brand's accent colour. The caption carries the argument.
- **Siblings:** the alert card (`alert`) adds a small badge, for news, deadlines and warnings.
  Bold text (`bold-text`) drops the polish and looks like a plain advert: use it for an offer or a blunt promise.
- **Watch for:** using it every day because it is the easiest to make. It is the format most likely to become wallpaper.

### 5. Note-style card
- **Use it for:** personal lessons, short lists, things that sound better jotted than designed.
- **Best on:** Instagram and Facebook, from a person. Good for the `five-lessons` and `bullet-insight` shapes.
- **Rules:** plain type, a title, three to six lines. It should look made in a minute, and still
  be tidy enough to read at a glance.
- **Watch for:** a company voice. A note reads as a person's. From a company account it can feel false.

### 6. Post-screenshot style
- **Use it for:** one quotable line. An opinion, a rule of thumb, a sentence from the brand's own voice samples.
- **Best on:** Instagram and LinkedIn (where a text post cannot travel as an image otherwise), and Facebook.
- **Rules:** the name on the card is the identity that is posting. One or two lines. Never
  imitate another person's account, a real customer's post, or a platform's exact interface.
- **Watch for:** invented quotes. The words must be the brand's own.

### 7. Photo with text
- **Use it for:** the person, the place, the product. Trust, presence, a bridge toward video.
- **Best on:** Instagram and Facebook.
- **Rules:** approved photos only, from the brand kit. Text of 3 to 8 words, placed where the photo is calm.
  The Factory uses a different photo each time and never the same one twice in a row.
  Stock and generated pictures are allowed, people included, when the person running the brand
  asks for them. What is shown must not be passed off in words as something it is not: no stock
  or generated person is captioned as a named customer, and no quote or result is put beside one
  unless it is real.
- **Watch for:** small text on a busy photo. If it cannot be read at thumbnail size, it fails.

### 8. Plain screenshot
- **Use it for:** proof that something really happened: a kind message, a sold-out booking page, a page from the order book.
- **Best on:** X, Facebook, LinkedIn. Works as slide 2 of a carousel.
- **Rules:** real and unaltered. Remove names and private details unless there is clear permission. One arrow or circle at most.
- **Watch for:** anything recreated to look like a real screenshot. If it did not happen, it is not shown.

### The LinkedIn PDF
- **Use it for:** the same content as a branded carousel, on LinkedIn, where documents are the most saved format.
- **Rules:** 6 to 10 pages, type 24pt or larger, a recap page second from last, no link on any page.

## Choosing a format

Choose by what the post is for. Then check the platform. Then apply the rotation rule.

| The post's job | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Stop the scroll | Headline card or bold text, photo with text, split before and after |
| Teach, and earn the save | Branded carousel, LinkedIn PDF, note-style card |
| Prove it | Plain screenshot, stat card, split before and after |
| Take a position | Headline card, post-screenshot style |
| Be quoted or passed on | Post-screenshot style, stat card |
| Sound like a person | Note-style card, photo with text |

| Platform | Leans toward |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | LinkedIn PDF, stat card, plain screenshot. Tidy, designed formats suit the setting |
| Instagram | Everything works. Lead with carousels |
| Facebook | Photo with text, headline card, plain screenshot, split. Real pictures do best |
| X | Plain screenshot, post-screenshot style, stat card. Simple, readable at small size |

| Who is speaking | Leans toward |
|---|---|
| A person ("I") | More raw: note-style, photo with text, post-screenshot style |
| A company ("we") | More polished: carousel, stat card, split, headline card |

## The rotation rule

These are checks, not hopes. The planner applies them to every batch.

1. **Never the same format back to back** on one account and platform.
2. **Both ends, every week:** each account posts at least one polished format and at least one raw one.
3. **Start at about two polished posts for every raw one,** then adjust. A company account can go
   more polished. A person's account can go rawer.
4. **Cap the loud ones.** Headline cards at no more than a third of a week's images. Anything
   built on humour or imitation, once a batch at most.
5. **Choose first, rotate second.** Pick the format by job, voice and platform. Then check rule 1
   and move to the next best choice if it repeats.
6. **Photos rotate too.** Never the same base photo twice running on one account.
7. **With no approved photos,** rotate within the always-available set. It is
   wide enough to keep rules 1 and 2.

**A week with no key and no photos** (one account, five posts):

| Day | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Branded carousel | Teach. Polished |
| Tue | Post-screenshot style | One line worth repeating. Raw |
| Wed | Stat card | One real number. Polished |
| Thu | Note-style card | A short personal list. Raw |
| Fri | Split before and after | A contrast. Polished |

## Rules for every format

- **Readable at the size of a phone thumbnail.** If the main line cannot be read, it is not finished.
- **One idea per image.**
- **The brand's own colours and fonts,** from `brand-master.json` and `Brand/brand-kit/`.
- **Every number traces to the brand's files. Every quote is the brand's own words.**
- **Instagram always needs an image or a video.** The always-available set guarantees one.
- **Alt text on every image:** one plain sentence saying what it shows.
- **Learn from results.** Once numbers come in, shift the mix toward what works for each account. Keep the range.
