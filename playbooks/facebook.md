# Facebook rulebook

> last_researched: 2026-06-11 (as of June 2026). Confidence: high
> Basis: Facebook's owner publishes how its feed ranking works, and announces policy changes in
> its newsroom. Most of this rulebook rests on those pages. Engagement figures come from large
> third-party benchmark studies and are marked as such. Organic posts only, no adverts.
> Every example is invented. See `README.md`.

## 1. How reach works there now

**Start with the honest number.** A Page post reaches roughly one follower in a hundred
(large benchmark studies, early 2026), and views are falling year on year. A Page post left on its
own is seen by almost nobody. Real distribution comes from four places:

1. Shares and sends.
2. Groups.
3. Reels, which are recommended to people who do not follow you.
4. A person's profile running in Professional Mode.

Plan every post around at least one of those four.

**How the feed decides (from the platform's own documentation).** It gathers everything you
could be shown, reads signals about each post, predicts what you are likely to do with it, and
orders the results by score. The signals it names:

- Who posted, and your history with them.
- The format (photo, video, link) and which formats you tend to stop for.
- Whether people you know have interacted with it.
- **Time spent on the post**, including watching a video for 30 seconds or more.
- **Comments: how many, how long, and whether they are about the post's subject.**
- For links: how likely you are to click, and how long you would stay on the other site.
- Shares and saves.

A writer can influence three of those: time spent, real comments on the subject, and shares.
Any article quoting exact weights ("a share is worth N likes") is guessing. The order is sound:
shares and sends first, then comments, then reactions.

**Five changes from 2025 and 2026 that matter.**

| When | What changed | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | A stated push back toward friends and real people, including a friends-only tab | Identifiable people beat faceless brand posts |
| April 2025 | Spam crackdown: long captions stuffed with hashtags, captions that do not match the media, and fake engagement get reach cut to followers only | No hashtag blocks. The caption must describe the photo |
| June 2025 | Every uploaded video is now a Reel, with no length limit | Video is how you reach non-followers |
| March 2026 | Originality enforcement: re-uploads and lightly edited copies are demoted. Original work, or other material with real new analysis added, is rewarded | Your own photos, footage and thinking win |
| 2025 to 2026 | Recommended posts from accounts you do not follow now make up around half the feed or more | Video, and anything people pass on, can travel to strangers. A still post on a Page rarely does |

**Links.** By the platform's own reporting, 97 to 98 percent of post views in the United States
go to posts with no outside link. (Sources disagree on exactly which posts that figure covers.
Take the point and not the decimal: a post carrying a link is seen much less.) Benchmarks agree
that link posts sit at the bottom for engagement. Many marketers drop the link into the first
comment instead. That is a habit of the trade, not official advice. And since December 2025 the platform has been testing a limit of about two link
posts for Professional Mode profiles and Pages, unless they pay for verification. Links in
comments are exempt from that test.

**Groups.** Groups are ranked by a separate system. By most accounts a post inside a group is
seen by a much bigger share of its members than a Page post is by the Page's followers. The specific figures in
circulation come from weak sources. The direction is consistent everywhere.

**Professional Mode.** Switching a personal profile to Professional Mode makes its public posts
eligible to be recommended to non-followers, while keeping the advantage people have over brands.
If the brand has a person who posts, that profile is the main Facebook channel and the Page comes second.

## 2. First lines that earn the click

**The limit.** "See more" appears after two or three lines on a phone, so the opening 125
characters have to stand by themselves. That first sentence is also what earns time on the post, which is an official signal.

**The platform's own three kinds of opening** (from its creator guidance, written for Reels and
just as true for text):

| Kind | What it does | Invented example |
|---|---|---|
| A benefit promised | Leads with what the reader gets | "A softer crust on your home loaf, from one change to how you cool it." |
| A statement of what you will show | Says plainly what is coming | "Here is a 14 hour dough next to a 4 hour one, both cut open." |
| A question or invitation | Asks the reader to think or join in | "Runners over 40: which came first for you, the sore knee or the new shoes?" |

**The specificity rule.** Every opening carries a concrete noun (a place, a product, a kind of
person) and, where the brand really has one, a number. Openings that promise a shock and hide the
subject are both a demotion risk and a magnet for the wrong readers.

**The first image rule.** With a photo or a Reel, the interesting thing is in the first image or
the first frame. The cut loaf, the before and after, the chart. On Facebook the picture does the
opening's job.

More patterns with templates are in `hooks.md`.

## 3. Post skeletons

### A. The Photo With a Point (one photo or a short album)
*Use it when you have a real picture that carries the story: the product, the result, the before and after.*

```
[LINE 1, 125 characters or fewer: concrete noun plus a real number]
[LINE 2: what the photos show ("First photo is Tuesday's bake. Second is Friday's.")]

[BODY, 3 to 6 short lines, one idea each:
  what the situation was
  the ONE thing that changed, in plain words
  why it worked. Give the full explanation in the post.]

[CLOSE: a question that needs a real sentence in reply]
```
One strong photo now does about as well as an album. Do not build long albums for their own sake.

### B. The Counter Lesson (text status, from a person's profile)
*Use it when you have a hard-won lesson or a pattern you keep seeing, and no picture is needed.*

```
[LINE 1: the pattern, with a count ("I have taught 60 beginners this year. Almost all of them make the same first mistake.")]
[LINE 2: what it costs them]

[NUMBERED BODY, 3 to 5 lines. Each line is one complete thought.
  Move from what goes wrong, to what causes it, to the repair.
  End on the repair, in enough detail to try this evening.]

[ONE LINE worth passing on]
[CLOSE: a question answered from experience ("Which of these caught you out first?")]
```
No link, no hashtags. Its job is comments and sends. Text posts reach fewer people than they
did, but they sound like someone speaking, and that is what Facebook is leaning toward.

### C. The Show-Me Reel (15 to 30 seconds)
*Use it when you can demonstrate the point on camera. This is the format that reaches strangers.*

```
[0-3s    Say what they are about to see, with the thing already on screen and moving]
[3-15s   The demonstration. One claim, one demo. Plain narration. Text labels on screen.]
[15-25s  Why it happened, in one or two sentences]
[last 3s One ask: follow for the next one]
Caption: the first two lines of skeleton A. Captions on.
```
Longer is fine only when the demonstration holds attention all the way. Full detail is in `short-form-video.md`.

### D. The Group Answer
*Use it when someone in a community group has asked the question your brand can answer well.*

```
[Answer the question in the first line. No preamble, no introduction of yourself.]
[The full method, in 3 to 6 short lines. Hold nothing back.]
[One caution or exception]
[Offer to answer follow-ups in the thread. No link. No pitch.]
```
Follow each group's rules. Rewrite the post for the group instead of pasting it: pasted copies
are treated as unoriginal, and members notice. The aim is to be the name they recognise later.

**After posting, every time:**

1. Post from the person's profile first (Professional Mode), then share to the Page.
2. Rewrite for one or two relevant groups where the rules allow.
3. Reply to every real comment within the first hour.

## 4. Length, links, hashtags and calls to action

**Length.** There is no practical cap. The old advice that 40 to 80 characters is ideal comes
from studies before 2018 and should be ignored. What holds up: the first 125 characters work
alone, and a structured body of 200 to 600 characters does well when it teaches something.
Reels: 15 to 30 seconds by default.

**Links.** None in the caption. If one is needed, first comment, and not often (see the link test above).

**Hashtags.** Zero to two, or none. Never a block. Hashtags correlate very weakly with
engagement here, and stuffing them is a named spam signal. The brand's style setting wins.

**Calls to action.**

| Priority | The ask | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First | A comment prompt that needs a real sentence ("What did your first loaf look like?") | Comment length and relevance are ranking signals |
| Second | Ask them to pass it to one kind of person ("One for whoever you run with on Sundays") | Shares and sends are the strongest lever left |
| Rare | A soft sales line, in the first comment | The sale happens in the comments and messages, not at the click |
| Never | One-word bait ("comment YES") | Engagement bait is a demotion category |

## 5. Formats that work

In order, from early 2026 benchmarks:

1. **Reels.** The one format whose engagement rose, and the main recommendation surface. Demonstrations.
2. **Single photo or album.** Real photos do about a third better than text alone in one large study.
3. **Text status.** Down from 2025, still the best for relationship and trust, from a person's profile.
4. **Link posts.** Lowest of all. Avoid.

Facebook has no thread format. Use a single well-structured post instead, or a set of photos
with one point written under each.

## 6. What to avoid

- **Reach from the wrong people.** A generic tips post or a provocative one can travel through
  the recommendation system to people who will never buy, and it skews who sees your next posts.
  If one takes off with the wrong crowd, do not feed it. Go back to your subjects.
- **Links in the caption.** The most common business mistake on Facebook.
- **Engagement bait and shock openings.** A named demotion category since April 2025.
- **Hashtag blocks and captions that do not match the media.** A named spam signal.
- **The same post pasted into profile, Page and groups.** Demoted as unoriginal, and it reads as spam.
- **Re-uploaded or lightly edited material from someone else.** Borders, new captions and speed
  changes do not make it yours.
- **Holding the answer back.** "Comment a word and I will message you the rest" throws away the
  reading time, risks a bait penalty, and looks like a scam to a careful customer.
- **Page only.** Decide where a post will travel before you make it. If the answer is nowhere, skip it.

| Real photos, results and numbers | The proof in `Inputs/` and `Studio/`, the facts table in `Brand/business-profile.md` |

Decisions to make per brand:

- **Profile or Page?** A person identity posts first. A company-only brand leans harder on Reels and groups.
- **Which groups?** List the two or three where the brand's customers already talk. Read their rules.
- **What can be photographed this week?** Facebook rewards real pictures. Plan the photo before the caption.
- **Local brand?** Name the neighbourhood. Facebook favours what it predicts is personally
  relevant, and a named place is the quickest signal.
- **Regulated brand?** Describe, do not promise. Use only approved claims.
