# Shape: Myth vs Reality (`myth-vs-reality`)

> last_researched: 2026-06-11 (as of June 2026). Length: short. Identity: any
> The worked example is invented. See `../README.md`.

## What one idea becomes

One belief the audience holds, wrong or only half right, becomes **a clean correction**: what
people think, what is true, the one reason, and what to do instead. It is the fastest shape to
read and one of the most forwarded, because the reader usually knows someone who believes the myth.

Choose it when the contract holds a belief customers really express (look in the brand's
audience file) and a fact or example that settles it. One myth per post.

## The structure, beat by beat

1. **Opening (1 line).** Name the belief, or the person who holds it, in everyday words.
2. **Myth (1 line).** The belief, stated the way people actually say it. Quote marks help.
3. **Why people believe it (1 line).** Be fair. The reader may believe it too, and nobody
   forwards a post that calls them a fool.
4. **Reality (1 line).** What is true instead. Same length and form as the myth line.
5. **The reason (1 to 4 lines).** The single fact, number or example from the contract.
6. **Do this instead (1 line).** One action.
7. **Close (optional, 1 line).** A save or send prompt, or a question.

On the shortest platforms keep beats 2, 4 and 5 and drop the rest.

**The two key lines are a pair.** "Myth:" and "Reality:" should be the same length and rhythm, so
they can sit on the two halves of a split card.

## Length

| Platform | Length |
|---|---|
| X | One post within 280 characters: myth, reality, reason |
| Instagram | A two-slide or split card, plus a caption of 300 to 600 |
| Facebook | 200 to 400 characters, with the split card |
| LinkedIn | Possible at 400 to 700, but `bullet-insight` usually serves LinkedIn better |

## Which platforms it suits, and why

- **X:** a corrected belief with its evidence is one of the strongest openings there, and it fits one post.
- **Instagram:** the two key lines make a two-slide carousel or a split card, and slide 2 works
  alone, which suits how carousels are shown a second time.
- **Facebook:** short, picture-led, and easy to send to the friend who believes the myth.

Visual pairing: the split before and after card, or the two-slide correction in `../instagram.md`.

## Voice: a person or a company

- **A person (says "I").** "I believed this for years" is a strong line, only if the contract
  says it is true. A person can be a little blunter.
- **A company (says "we" and "you").** "You may have heard..." then "Here is what we see."
  Keep it friendly. A company correcting its customers can sound superior very quickly.

Both: attack the belief, never the believer. Never name a competitor as the source of the myth.

## The rule

**Fill it only from the contract you were given. Never invent a fact, number, quote or
customer.** The reality line must be something the brand can stand behind. On a regulated brand,
say "often" and "rarely" where that is the truth. Do not replace one absolute with another.

## Worked example (Maple & Main Bakery, invented)

Platform: Instagram. Identity: Maple & Main (a company). Visual: a two-slide correction.

- Slide 1: "Sourdough takes all day."
- Slide 2: "It takes 14 hours. You are busy for 20 minutes of them."

```text
Think sourdough needs a whole day at home? It needs a whole day. It does not need you.

Myth: you have to be in the kitchen from morning to night.

It is a fair thing to believe. Most recipes list a dozen steps.

Reality: for nearly all of that time the dough is working and you are not.

Here is our own house loaf, timed.
Mix: 5 minutes.
Four folds: 1 minute each.
Shape: 5 minutes.
Bake: 45 minutes, and the oven does that part.

Mix after dinner, fold during the evening, bake the next morning.

Send this to the friend who says they do not have time to bake.

#sourdough #homebaking #breadmaking
```

The same idea on X, within 280 characters:

```text
Myth: sourdough takes all day.

Reality: it takes 14 hours, and you are busy for about 20 minutes of them.

Mix: 5 minutes.
Four folds: a minute each.
Shape: 5 minutes.
The oven does the rest.

Mix after dinner. Bake at breakfast.
```

What to notice: the myth is said the way a customer would say it. The belief is treated as
reasonable. The evidence is the brand's own timings. There is one ask, and it is a send.
