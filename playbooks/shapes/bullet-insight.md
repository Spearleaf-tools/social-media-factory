# Shape: Bullet Insight (`bullet-insight`)

> last_researched: 2026-06-11 (as of June 2026). Length: medium. Identity: any
> The worked example is invented. See `../README.md`.

## What one idea becomes

One sharp observation becomes **a fast post with one thought per line**: the insight, the usual
way of doing things that it replaces, a handful of short parallel examples that prove it, and
the insight again as a rule worth remembering. It reads in twenty seconds and is built to be
bookmarked and quoted.

Choose it when the contract holds one real insight and at least three short, concrete examples
of it. If the examples need a paragraph each, use `three-tips` or `five-lessons` instead.

## The structure, beat by beat

1. **The insight, flat (1 line).** A plain statement. No build-up. This line must work alone as the opening.
2. **What people usually do instead (2 to 4 short lines).** The habit the insight replaces. A tiny scene is best.
3. **Why that fails (1 or 2 lines).** Short. This is the turn.
4. **The bullets (3 to 6 lines).** Each is one standalone example or instruction. All in the same
   form, so the eye runs down them. Put the strongest first and the most surprising last.
5. **The tally (1 or 2 lines, optional).** What it took. Often a contrast: what changed, what did not.
6. **The rule (1 line).** The insight restated as something to remember. This is the line people quote.
7. **A question (1 line, optional).** Only if you want answers.

**One sentence per line.** No line longer than about 15 words. A blank line between beats. White
space is what makes this shape fast.

**Parallel bullets.** If the first bullet starts with a quoted name, they all do. If the first
is an instruction, they all are. Broken parallelism is what makes a list feel machine-written.

## Length

| Platform | Length |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 600 to 1,100 characters |
| X | One post within 280 characters (insight, three bullets, rule). Or a thread of 3 or 4 posts |
| Facebook | 400 to 700 |
| Instagram | A note-style card or a post-screenshot style card carrying the insight, with the full text as the caption |

## Which platforms it suits, and why

- **X:** short lines and a quotable rule are the native style, and a dense list earns bookmarks.
- **LinkedIn:** one thought per line holds attention on a phone, and reading time is what LinkedIn rewards.
- **Instagram and Facebook:** the insight line alone makes a strong headline card or post-screenshot style card.

## Voice: a person or a company

- **A person (says "I").** "I used to label loaves the way bakers talk." The insight is something you noticed.
- **A company (says "we" and "you").** "We renamed everything." Then hand the rule to "you".

Both: no warm-up, no moralising. State it, show it, stop.

## The rule

**Fill it only from the contract you were given. Never invent a fact, number, quote or
customer.** Every bullet is a real example from the brand. If a bullet has a result in it ("sold
out first for six Saturdays"), that result is in the contract.

## Worked example (Maple & Main Bakery, invented)

Platform: LinkedIn. Identity: Maple & Main (a company). Visual: a post-screenshot style card with the first line.

```text
Customers cannot ask for what they cannot name.

We used to label loaves the way bakers talk.
"Country levain, 80 percent hydration."
People pointed and said "that one".

Pointing does not travel.
Nobody tells a friend to go and buy "that one".

So we renamed every loaf by what it is for.

- "The toast loaf." It sold out first for six Saturdays running.
- "The soup loaf." People now buy it on cold days without being told to.
- "The sandwich loaf." Parents stopped asking which one the kids would eat.
- "The cheese loaf." It has no cheese in it. It goes with cheese.

We changed no recipes.
We changed four labels.

If a customer cannot say it, they cannot recommend it.

What do your customers call the thing you sell?
```

The same idea on X, within 280 characters:

```text
Customers cannot ask for what they cannot name.

We renamed our loaves by what they are for.
The toast loaf. The soup loaf. The sandwich loaf.

Same recipes. Four new labels.
The toast loaf sold out first, six Saturdays running.

If they cannot say it, they cannot recommend it.
```

What to notice: the whole idea is in the opening line. The scene is three lines long. The bullets are
built identically. The tally is a contrast. The rule is short enough to repeat.
