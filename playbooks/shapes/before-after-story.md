# Shape: Before and After Story (`before-after-story`)

> last_researched: 2026-06-11 (as of June 2026). Length: medium. Identity: **person**
> The worked example is invented. See `../README.md`.

## What one idea becomes

One real change a person went through becomes **a short first-person story**: where I was, what
it was costing, the moment it turned, what I did differently, where things are now, and what I
would say to someone still at the start. It is the most personal shape, and the one that most
needs real material.

Choose it **only** when the contract holds all three of these:

- a real **before** (a time, a place, a state of affairs),
- a real **after** (a number, or a change anyone could observe),
- the **time between** them.

If any of the three is missing, this shape is not available. Choose `five-lessons` or `problem-agitate-solve`.

## Why a person only

This shape is marked `identity: person` in `index.json`. A story of change told as "we" reads as
a case study or an advert, and loses what makes it work: one person admitting where they
started. For a company identity, tell a result with the Shown Result skeleton in the platform
rulebook, or use `problem-agitate-solve`.

## The structure, beat by beat

1. **The before, as a dated scene (2 or 3 lines).** When and where. What you were doing, or what
   you believed. Open on the most concrete detail.
2. **What it was costing (1 or 2 lines).** In real terms. A figure if the contract has one.
3. **The turn (2 to 4 lines).** The specific event or decision. A day, a conversation, a small
   disaster. Then the realisation, in one plain sentence.
4. **What I did differently (up to 3 numbered lines).** Concrete actions someone else could copy.
5. **The after (2 or 3 lines).** The real change, with the time it took. If it has held, say so.
   If it is modest, say that too.
6. **To someone at the "before" (1 or 2 lines).** One piece of advice, specific and small.
7. **A question (1 line, optional).**

**Proportion.** The before and the turn get the most words. Readers recognise themselves in the
before. The after only proves that the story is worth telling.

**Honest scale.** A change from 22 to 3 is a good story. It does not need to be "transformed
everything". Write the true size of the change. Understatement is believed.

## Length

| Platform | Length |
|---|---|
| Instagram | Caption of 600 to 1,000 characters, with a split before and after card or a photo with text |
| LinkedIn | 700 to 1,200 |
| Facebook | 400 to 800, from the person's own profile |
| X | A thread of 4 or 5 posts. Post 1 is the before and the after in one line |

## Which platforms it suits, and why

- **Instagram:** the before and after is a natural split card, and personal stories get sent to
  friends who are at the "before".
- **LinkedIn:** a real story with a real figure earns reading time, and the numbered actions earn the save.
- **Facebook:** sounds like someone speaking, which Facebook favours, and draws "me too" replies.

## Voice: a person or a company

- **A person (says "I").** Required. Write it the way the person tells it aloud. Read
  `Brand/voice-samples.md` first, and keep their rough edges.
- **A company.** Not available for this shape (see above).

Keep it free of triumph. The turn is usually a mistake noticed, not a victory won. Thank people
who helped only if the contract names them and they have agreed.

## The rule

**Fill it only from the contract you were given. Never invent a fact, number, quote or
customer.** Most of all here: never invent or enlarge a before, never round the after upward,
never shorten the time it took. On a regulated brand, a personal story of change can imply an
outcome claim. Follow the brand's compliance settings, and use only approved stories.

## Worked example (Maple & Main Bakery, invented)

Platform: Instagram. Identity: Ava Lindqvist, head baker (a person). Visual: a split card, "22 a night" on one side and "3 a night" on the other.

```text
In March two years ago I was binning 22 loaves a night.

I would lock up at six, fill two sacks with good bread, and call it the cost of a full counter.
It was costing us about a fifth of everything we baked.

The turn was a Tuesday.
A regular asked for a seeded loaf at 9am and we had sold out.
That same night I threw away eleven white tins.
We were not baking too much. We were baking the wrong things on the wrong days.

So I changed three things.

1. A whiteboard by the oven. Every night we write what sold out and what was left.
2. We bake from last week's board, not from habit.
3. Anything left at 4pm goes to the shelter on Maple Street, not the bin.

Six weeks later we were down to three loaves a night. It has stayed there.

If you are where I was, write it down for one week before you change anything.
The board will tell you what to do.

What do you throw away every day without counting?

#bakerylife #foodwaste #sourdough
```

What to notice: the before opens on a number and a scene. The turn is one ordinary Tuesday. The
actions could be copied by any shop. The after gives the time it took and says it has held. The
advice is small enough to do this week.
