# Shape: Five Questions (`five-questions`)

> last_researched: 2026-06-11 (as of June 2026). Length: medium. Identity: any
> The worked example is invented. See `../README.md`.

## What one idea becomes

One subject the audience has lived becomes **five questions that start a real conversation**,
with the brand answering one of them first. The post gives little and asks a lot, so it only
works when the questions are good and the brand turns up in the comments afterwards.

Choose it when the audience has stories worth hearing, the brand genuinely wants the answers,
and someone will reply to every comment. Long, on-subject comments are a ranking signal on every
platform. They are also the best audience research a brand can get.

**This is not engagement bait.** Bait asks for a word ("comment YES"). This shape asks for a
sentence or a story, which cannot be given without thinking. If a question can be answered in
one word, rewrite it.

## The structure, beat by beat

1. **Framing (1 or 2 lines).** Why you are asking, and who you are asking. Name the kind of person.
2. **The five questions,** numbered. Each is 15 words or fewer, open, and answerable only from
   the reader's own experience. Spread them across a range:
   1. An easy memory. Anyone can answer it, and it warms the room.
   2. A practical habit or preference.
   3. A choice between two things, with "and why".
   4. A mistake, or a time it went wrong.
   5. A hope or a plan.
3. **Your own answer to ONE of them (2 to 4 lines).** From the contract. Choose the question that
   needs the most courage, usually number 4. This shows the kind of reply you are hoping for and
   earns the right to ask.
4. **The invitation (1 line).** Pick a number and answer it. Say that you read the replies. Then do.

**Number them.** "Pick a number" lowers the effort. People answer with "3:" and then write a
paragraph.

**After posting.** Reply to every answer, with a follow-up question where you can. Replies to
replies extend reach more than the post does. Note the best answers: they are future topics,
and they belong in the brand's audience file.

## Length

| Platform | Length |
|---|---|
| Facebook | 400 to 800 characters |
| LinkedIn | 500 to 900 |
| Instagram | A note-style card with the five questions, and the framing plus your own answer as the caption |
| X | Not as five. Ask one question per post there |

## Which platforms it suits, and why

- **Facebook:** comment count, length and relevance are documented ranking signals, and it works
  well adapted for a community group.
- **LinkedIn:** substantial comments and reply threads are the strongest active signal, and
  questions about working life get long answers.
- **Instagram:** possible as a card, but comments carry less weight there than saves and sends.

## Voice: a person or a company

- **A person (says "I").** "I will go first." The answer is your own story.
- **A company (says "we").** "We will go first, on number 4." A company admitting a mistake in
  its own answer is disarming, and makes a human reply easy.

Both: the questions are asked the way you would ask across a table. No survey language.

## The rule

**Fill it only from the contract you were given. Never invent a fact, number, quote or
customer.** The brand's own answer is a true story from its files. If the contract holds no
story for any of the five questions, change the questions until it does.

## Worked example (Maple & Main Bakery, invented)

Platform: Facebook. Identity: Maple & Main (a company). Visual: a note-style card listing the five questions.

```text
We talk about bread all day. We would rather hear about yours.

Five questions for anyone who has baked a loaf at home, even once.

1. What did your very first loaf look like?
2. What is the one piece of kit you would not give up?
3. Packet yeast or sourdough starter, and why?
4. What happened the time it went really wrong?
5. What do you want to be able to bake by Christmas?

We will go first, on number 4.
Our first wholesale order was 60 loaves, and we forgot the salt in every one.
We found out when the customer rang us. We baked all 60 again that night.

Pick a number and tell us. We read every answer.
```

What to notice: the first question is easy and the fourth takes courage. Every question needs a
sentence, not a word. The brand's own answer is specific and a little embarrassing, which makes
an honest reply feel safe. There is one invitation.
