# Short-form video rulebook

> last_researched: 2026-06-11 (as of June 2026). Confidence: high on mechanics, medium on thresholds
> Covers vertical video under about 60 seconds: Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels.
> Basis: public statements by the platforms about their own ranking, the metrics they show
> creators, and third-party retention studies. The order of the signals is well supported. The
> exact thresholds (what counts as "good" retention) are third-party estimates: check them
> against your own numbers. Every example is invented. See `README.md`.

## 1. How reach works there now

**Every platform runs the same loop: test, read, widen or stop.** Each new video first goes to
a limited group. How that group behaves decides what happens next. If they stay and respond,
a larger group sees it, and so on in waves. If they leave, it stops. Follower count is not a direct ranking
factor. Each video competes alone.

**What each platform reads.**

| Platform | The signals that matter most | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | Watch time (seconds, share completed, rewatches). Sends per reach, the strongest signal for reaching new people. Likes per reach, which matters more for existing followers | Confirmed publicly by Instagram's leadership |
| TikTok | Watch time and completion dominate. Saves and shares now count above likes | Since late 2025 new videos are tested on followers first, and that result opens or closes the wider feed. Longer videos (60 to 180 seconds) are rewarded when completion holds |
| YouTube Shorts | "Viewed vs swiped away": of the people shown the Short, the share who watched. Then average share viewed and replays | Shown in the channel analytics. A large independent study of billions of views found winners at 70 to 90 percent viewed, with little distribution under 60 |
| Facebook Reels | Broadly the same as Instagram Reels | The platform publishes little that is specific |

**About Shorts view counts.** From 31 March 2025 a view is logged whenever a Short starts
playing, so totals jumped overnight. The platform said this did not change how Shorts are recommended. Judge Shorts by
viewed versus swiped away and by retention, never by raw views.

**In one sentence.** Holding people through the opening three seconds gets a video past the
first gate. Finishing, saving and sending get it past the second. For a brand, saves and sends
are also the signals closest to real interest: "I kept this for the weekend", "I passed this to
the person who deals with it".

## 2. First lines that earn the watch

A video opening has three layers. All three must make the same promise.

| Layer | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Spoken | The opening sentence you say | The promise or the open question, in two to three seconds. Skip the hello and the preamble |
| Visual | The first frame and the first movement | Already in motion. The interesting object on screen from frame one |
| Text | 3 to 7 words on screen in frame one | Sharpens the spoken line. Essential for silent viewers |

**Why three seconds.** The largest single drop in every retention graph is at the start.
Third-party data associates holding roughly 65 percent or more of viewers through the first three
seconds with several times the impressions, and losing more than a third with a weak opening.
Treat those figures as direction.

**Sound.** Around half of video on Instagram is watched without sound, by the platform's own
account. (The older "85 percent" figure comes from a different platform and era. Ignore it.)
Design for silence either way: if the opening makes no sense muted, it is not finished. For a
brand that teaches, your own voice is the right audio. Trending sounds help entertainment formats
far more than they help you.

**Spoken patterns** (templates and visual patterns are in `hooks.md`):

| Pattern | Invented example |
|---|---|
| The result first | "This loaf took 14 hours and four minutes of actual work. Here are the four minutes." |
| A belief corrected | "Your knee pain is probably not about your knee." |
| A direct promise with a time on it | "In the next 30 seconds I will show you how to tell if your starter is ready." |
| A question that picks the viewer | "Started running after 40 and your calves hate you?" |
| A live test | "Let us see what happens if I skip the second proof. Same dough, two tins." |

**The agreement test.** Watch your first three seconds with the sound off. The picture alone must
carry the promise. Then watch with sound. Voice and picture must reinforce each other, not compete.
What the viewer sees first should prove what they hear first.

## 3. Post skeletons

**The retention curve to plan for.** Viewers fall away sharply in the opening three seconds when
the start is weak. A slower leak follows about 40 to 60 percent of the way through, when a video
loses pace before it delivers.
Counter both: a strong opening, and a visual change every three to five seconds (a cut, a zoom,
a new text card, a new angle).

### 1. Result, Reason, Your Turn (30 to 45 seconds). The default.
*Use it when you have a real result and can show what caused it.*

```
0-3s     OPENING. The result on screen plus the spoken result-first line.
3-8s     WHY IT MATTERS. One sentence, to this viewer.
8-25s    THE REASON, SHOWN. A demonstration, a close-up, an over-the-shoulder shot.
         Two or three steps. Label each one on screen. Change the shot every 3 to 5 seconds.
25-35s   SO WHAT. Put it in the viewer's terms: time, money, comfort, fewer mistakes.
35-45s   ONE ASK, then a final frame that matches the first so the loop replays cleanly.
```

### 2. The Held Answer (25 to 40 seconds)
*Use it when you are correcting a common belief and have evidence to stack.*

```
0-3s     OPENING. The belief, contradicted, with a picture that contradicts it too.
3-10s    OPEN THE QUESTION. Say there is one thing that matters more. Do not name it yet.
10-28s   THE EVIDENCE. Real examples, 3 to 5 seconds each.
28-38s   THE ANSWER. Name it plainly. Add a single line on doing it.
38-40s   ONE ASK.
```
The unanswered question carries viewers through the middle sag. The answer has to be as specific
as the opening made it sound, or the video will be remembered as a trick.

### 3. Watch Me Try It (40 to 60 seconds)
*Use it when you can run a small test on camera and the outcome is not obvious.*

```
0-3s     OPENING. Put the test as a challenge or a question.
3-30s    THE ATTEMPT, in something close to real time. Stay on the shot when the result arrives.
         Waiting for it is what holds people. Talk it through simply.
30-45s   THE VERDICT, and a one-line reason.
45-60s   A SINGLE STEP for the viewer to take now. Then the ask.
```

### 4. Side by Side (20 to 35 seconds)
*Use it when two versions of the same thing can be compared at a glance.*

```
0-3s     OPENING. Both versions on screen at once, identically framed. Text names the difference.
3-20s    WALK THE DIFFERENCES. Two or three, one at a time, each pointed at.
20-30s   WHICH, AND WHEN. The honest answer, including when the "worse" one is fine.
30-35s   ONE ASK.
```
Contrast is understood in a second, which makes this the easiest format to open well.

**Beats that apply to all four.**

- Pay off a promise within 6 to 10 seconds of making it, or make a smaller promise.
- Never let five seconds pass with no visual change.
- Keep your hands busy where you can. A person doing something holds the eye better than a person talking.
- Show the end state early. "How did that happen?" is what keeps people watching the process.
- Use the last two seconds to loop back to the first frame, or to set up the next video.

## 4. Length, links, hashtags and calls to action

**Length.** Match length to the size of the idea, then check retention.

| Platform | Guide (2026 data, directional) |
|---|---|
| TikTok | Videos of 21 to 34 seconds draw the most engagement. Up to 60 for teaching, provided nothing in it is filler. Longer only if completion holds |
| Reels | 30 to 90 seconds by the platform's guidance. 30 to 45 is the useful middle for teaching |
| Shorts | 30 to 60. Small ideas at 15 to 30 get the highest completion |

Rough yardsticks: under 15 seconds should hold 60 to 70 percent average retention. 30 to 60
seconds should hold 40 to 50 percent, and above 55 is strong. Default for a brand that teaches: 30 to 45 seconds.

**Links.** None of these formats carries a live link in the caption. The route is profile, then
link, or comments and messages. Keep the hard pitch out of the video and put the business ask in
the caption or a pinned comment.

**Hashtags.**

- **Instagram:** five at most, as labels. They do not add reach.
- **TikTok:** still useful for classification and search. Three to five specific ones. Skip the generic ones.
- **Shorts:** the title and the first line of the description do the work. Hashtags are minor.
- **Everywhere: say the subject out loud.** The platforms transcribe audio. If the video is about
  sourdough starter, the words "sourdough starter" should be spoken in it.

**Calls to action.**

- **One per video.** Two asks get neither.
- **Do not leave the only ask to the last second.** By then most viewers have gone. In a 30 to 60
  second video, place a light ask after the main payoff and repeat it briefly at the end. Under
  15 seconds, the end plus the caption is enough.
- **Prefer the asks the platforms reward:** "Save this for [a specific moment]" and "Send this to
  [a specific kind of person]".

**Captions and on-screen text.** Not optional. Use captions that highlight word by word, plus
short text cards of 3 to 7 words. Never a paragraph on screen: text-heavy frames lose viewers.

## 5. Formats that work

- **Vertical, 9 by 16, filmed for the phone.** No watermark from another app. Instagram holds those back.
- **Demonstration with hands in frame.** The strongest teaching format.
- **Screen recording or over-the-shoulder.** For anything that happens on a screen or a page.
- **Before and after, split screen.** Doubles as the visual opening.
- **Voice-over on process footage.** When the person would rather not be on camera.
- **Talking to camera.** Works when the first frame already has movement or an object in it, and
  the cuts are frequent. A person settling into a chair is the weakest opening there is.
- **One master cut for all four platforms**, with the caption and hashtags written per platform.
  The platforms weigh the same signals differently. They do not want different videos.

## 6. What to avoid

- **Reach from the wrong people.** A broad, sensational opening pulls viewers who will never be
  customers. The platform then models your audience on them and sends your next videos the same way.
  Score a video on how long people stayed, how many kept or forwarded it, who visited the
  profile and what the comments say. Views come last.
- **An opening the video cannot pay off.** It lifts the first three seconds and then collapses in
  the middle. Deliver something as specific as what you promised.
- **Jargon in the opening.** Trade terms lose the customer and attract other people in your trade.
  Use the words in your audience file.
- **Slow starts.** Logo cards, greetings, "so today", background before the point.
- **Saying it when you could show it.** "Proofing time matters", said to camera, gets scrolled
  past. Two loaves cut open next to each other get kept.
- **Several asks, or one ask hidden at the very end.**
- **Walls of text on screen.**
- **Trends that do not fit the brand.** A little extra discovery, paid for in trust and in who the
  platform thinks you are for.

| Why people would send or save it | `why-things-spread.md` |

Decisions to make per brand:

- **What can you demonstrate in 30 seconds?** List ten things. That list is the first month.
- **Who is the "send this to" person?** Name them in the ask.
- **One repeatable series.** The same opening format and framing each time, with a new subject
  inside. Viewers learn to recognise it, and it is quicker to make.
- **Regulated brand?** A demonstration is not a promise. Say what you are showing, avoid outcome
  claims, and include any standing disclaimer the brand uses.

**How to tell a video worked** (yardsticks, check against your own history):

- About 65 percent or more still watching at three seconds. For Shorts, 70 percent or more viewed versus swiped.
- About 50 percent average retention on a 30 to 45 second video.
- Saves and sends per reach rising across a batch.
- Comments that ask real questions ("how long do you proof yours?") rather than generic praise.
- Profile visits and messages. That is the conversion line.
